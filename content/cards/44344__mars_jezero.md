# Chocolate Mountains and Salton Sea (California, USA)

This 1° cell lies in the Colorado Desert of southeastern California, east of the Salton Sea, and includes part of the Chocolate Mountains [2]. It ranks 49th for Jezero outside the known analogs, with 0.72 [1].

**What is known.** Rainfall is typically 100 to 150 mm a year [2]. Water and ice is the best dimension (0.94): almost no snow or ice and little precipitation [1]. Life is documented: 158 NCBI records of halophiles and xerophiles, mostly from hypersaline sediment around the Salton Sea and from desert soils [1]. We did not find the cell described as a Mars analog; the nearest declared one is Death Valley (Ubehebe Crater), about 430 km away [1][4].

**What it could be used for (hypothesis).** Rehearsing the search for life in salty sediments and dry soils [1].

**Where the analogy breaks down.** Much of the range lies inside the Chocolate Mountain Aerial Gunnery Range, a Marine Corps bombing range closed to the public [2][3]. The mountains are rugged [2], but the cell averages 2.2° of slope and 44 m of roughness, against 2.8° and 64 m at Jezero [1]; flat basins probably hide the steep ground (hypothesis). Minimum temperature is 7 °C, versus −80 °C at Jezero, and dust optical depth is 0.03, against 0.45 [1].

**What to check in the field.** Whether any life records come from the mountains themselves, and whether access is possible.
