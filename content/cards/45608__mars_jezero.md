# Turkestan plains, northern Afghanistan

This 1° cell lies in northern Afghanistan, on the plains south of the Amu Darya river and near Kunduz [2]. It ranks 59th for Jezero (58th outside the known analogs), with 0.72 [1].

**What is known.** The northern foothills drop onto stony plains of 300 to 400 m with loess deposits and salt pans [2]. Dust storms sweep northern Afghanistan: NASA photographed one in June 2011 [3]. We did not find this cell described as a Mars analog. The nearest declared analog is Río Tinto (Spain), about 6,500 km away [1][4].

**What it could be used for (hypothesis).** Testing instruments and rover operations where terrain, dust and wind together resemble Jezero. Terrain (0.89) and dust and wind (0.91) are its best dimensions: dust optical depth is 0.18 against 0.45, and wind 3.8 m/s against 4 m/s [1].

**Where the analogy breaks down.** The minimum temperature is −1.6 °C, versus −80 °C at Jezero [1]. The satellite gives 362 mm of precipitation per year, more than the 100 to 200 mm the literature gives for the driest northern plains [1][2]; the cell may include wetter foothills (hypothesis). Water and ice scores 0.85 mostly because snow cover is nearly absent [1]. Dust and wind come from a reanalysis [1]. It has no NCBI records, perhaps because nobody has sampled it.

**What to check in the field.** Dust composition, weather-station data and whether fieldwork is possible.
